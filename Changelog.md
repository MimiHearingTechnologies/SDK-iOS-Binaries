#### Changed:
- The SDK is now built and tested with Xcode 27 on iOS 27. The SDK frameworks needed no functional changes; the repository's test host apps adopted the UIKit scene-based life cycle, which apps built with the iOS 27 SDK are required to use. Integrators building with Xcode 27 must adopt the scene-based life cycle in their own app — the SDK is started from `application(_:didFinishLaunchingWithOptions:)` as before, see `Docs/MimiSDK/Basics.md`.
- Internal: cleaned up the Xcode 27 implicit-strong-`self`-capture compiler warnings. No functional change. The remaining `#IsolatedConformances` warnings need `MimiProcessingParameter` to refine `SendableMetatype`, which would raise the integration floor to Xcode 26 and is deferred to 13.0.0.
- XCFrameworks built with Xcode 27 keep the classic module-interface spelling (`Swift.Int` rather than `Swift::Int`), so they can still be integrated with Xcode 26.0–26.2, whose compilers cannot parse the new module selectors Xcode 27 emits by default.
- Internal: worked around a Swift 6.4 optimizer crash in the Mixpanel analytics transport that prevented archiving MimiCoreKit with Xcode 27. No functional change.
- Internal: the user-flow navigation tests now wait for the navigation transition to finish instead of a fixed delay that iOS 27 left too little margin on, and a view-model test that asserted a debounce the view model no longer has was removed. No functional change.



#### Fixed:
- The Profile cards ignored the horizontal safe area. The cards were sized to the full width of the screen, so on a display with a horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — they spread under the status bar and its clock and Wi-Fi indicator covered the Sound Personalization toggle. The cards are now sized to fit within the safe area.
- The content of a user-flow step was centred on the screen instead of the safe area. The step's header and footer are laid out within the safe area, but the content was centred on the full view, so on a display with an asymmetric horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — the content of every test, authentication and onboarding step sat off-centre from its title and buttons and was squeezed narrower than them. The content is now centred on the safe area like the rest of the step.
- The footer of a user-flow step stretched its action and text buttons across the full width of the screen on any wide phone-idiom display, such as the iPhone Duo, while the header above it stayed capped at 432 pt and centred. `MimiNavigationStepFooterView` only capped its width on iPad. The footer's buttons are now capped at the same 432 pt and centred on wide phones too; narrower phones are unaffected, and, as before, the buttons span the full width above the keyboard while it's visible.
- On iOS 15, the sheets the hearing test's setup opens to explain headphone troubleshooting, audio effects, accessibility settings and Do Not Disturb had no close button. The button was added to the navigation item of the `UIHostingController` that showed the SwiftUI content, and on iOS 15 that controller replaces the navigation item's bar button items with its own, empty, toolbar the first time it lays out. The SwiftUI content is now embedded in a container view controller that owns the navigation item, so the close button stays on every iOS version.
- The analytics delivery queue could be read while a flush was modifying it. Events are enqueued on the thread that tracks them and dequeued by a flush on a background queue, and the collections were shared without synchronisation, so an inspection of the deliveries during a flush could crash with an index out of range. The queue and each delivery's status are now guarded by a lock.
- The sign-up consent step ran under the horizontal safe area. A user-flow step that sets `layoutContentToSafeArea` to `false` was laid out to the edges of the view on both axes, so on a display with a horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — the consent text and its Accept and Decline buttons sat off-centre and ran under the status bar. Opting out of the safe area now only lets a step run under the top and bottom insets; it stays within the horizontal safe area unless it also sets `requiresFullWidth` to `true`. Integrators with their own steps that opt out and need to span the full width must now set `requiresFullWidth` as well.
- SDK alerts were centred on the screen instead of the safe area. `MimiAlertController` centred its 300 pt alert card on the full view, so on a display with a horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — every SDK alert sat off-centre and its trailing edge could run under the status bar. The card is now centred within the horizontal safe area and, should the safe area ever be narrower than the card, shrinks to stay inside it.
- The device-authorization overlay ignored the horizontal safe area. The TV-authorization step shows its prompt, artwork, Scan button, scan instructions, progress indicator and success message in an overlay over a full-bleed camera preview, and that content was pinned to the edges of the screen, so on a display with a horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — it sat off-centre and its trailing edge ran under the status bar. The overlay content now stays within the horizontal safe area; the camera preview and the overlay's blurred background remain full-bleed.
- The text of an alert banner could run under the horizontal safe area. `MimiAlertBanner` spans the full width of the view it is shown in and inset its text a fixed 16 pt from its own edges, so when shown in a view that extends under a horizontal safe-area inset — such as on the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — the text ran under the status bar. The banner background stays full-bleed, its text is now inset from the horizontal safe area as well, and the banner grows to fit text that wraps onto more lines as a result. The SDK's own sign-up and reset-password banners already sit within the safe area thanks to the user-flow fixes above in this release.
- The audio effects explainer that the hearing test's setup opens from \"Learn more\" ignored the horizontal safe area. Its text was inset a fixed 24 pt from the edges of the screen, so on a display with a horizontal safe-area inset — such as the iPhone Duo, whose status bar occupies a column down the right edge — the text ran under the status bar and sat off-centre. The text is now inset from the horizontal safe area.
- The \"Last test\" date on the PTT test results screen ignored the user's locale. It was formatted with a fixed day.month.year pattern, so it appeared as, for example, 17.09.2026 in every language and region, and disagreed with the date on the Hearing ID card. Both dates on the results screen now use the locale's short date format, matching the Hearing ID card: for example 9/17/26 in US English, 17/09/2026 in British English and 2026/9/17 in Simplified Chinese.
- A hearing test result whose issues the SDK could not describe left the user at a dead end. When the result service reported only issue codes this version of the SDK has no message for, the Profile showed no issues card at all, and the \"Results may be inaccurate\" banner on the PTT test results screen could not be tapped. The Profile now shows the issues card with \"Your results may be inaccurate\", and the card and the banner open a sheet with that message and an active \"Test again\" button, without the \"We found 0 issues\" line. When a result has both describable and undescribable issues, the card now counts only the describable ones, matching the sheet, and takes its colour from them.
- The app could crash after a hearing test while the new preset was being sent to the device. A processing parameter's `apply`, `load` and `synchronizeApplicator` were not serialised, so when the recommendation engine's reload of the preset, the Profile's fine-tuning card and the host app drove the same parameter at the same time, their concurrent updates to the parameter's state corrupted memory and could invoke the applicator concurrently for the same parameter. A parameter now runs these calls, and the suspension and resumption of Sound Personalization and Voice Clarity, one at a time in the order they were made, the applicator is never invoked concurrently for the same parameter, and a load that finds its value already applied no longer invokes the applicator again. Parameter values and states can no longer be published out of order either, and a failed application reached through a load reports a single failure instead of two. A call that is waiting its turn and whose task is cancelled throws `CancellationError` at once rather than after the call ahead of it completes, so a host that bounds an application with its own deadline is not held up by an applicator that never returns.
- The in-memory store behind the personalization controller's cache was not thread-safe. Reading it on one thread while another wrote to it could corrupt it and crash. Every access is now serialised.
- Logging in could crash if the log-in screen was dismissed while the request was still in flight. When the request completed, the log-in step reached for its flow, footer and content, which had been released with the screen. The step now only reports the result to analytics in that case.
- The Profile could crash with an index out of range while its cards were being replaced. A change of Profile state swaps the cards at once but reloads the card list a moment later, and a layout pass in between, such as the one forced when the Profile switches from the onboarding introduction back to the cards right after a log-out, asked for a card that no longer existed. The card list now tolerates that layout pass.
- An applicator timeout did not fire for an applicator that does not observe task cancellation. The timeout cancelled the applicator's task and then waited for it to finish, so an applicator parked on a device reply that never came — for example a continuation that is only resumed by a transport callback — never returned and `applyTimeoutExceeded` was never thrown; with the serialisation introduced above, every later `apply`, `load` and `synchronizeApplicator` on that parameter, and the suspension and resumption of its feature, then queued behind it for good. The timeout now fires after the configured duration regardless of the applicator: the application fails with `applyTimeoutExceeded`, the parameter moves on to its next operation, and the applicator's task is cancelled but no longer awaited. Cancelling the task that called `apply` while the applicator is running now also returns at once, with `CancellationError`, whether or not a timeout is set, and a call made from a task that is already cancelled no longer invokes the applicator at all. An applicator that keeps running after its application timed out or was cancelled is still never invoked concurrently: the next application waits for it to return before invoking it again, and that wait counts towards the next application's own timeout, so applications behind an applicator that never returns each fail with `applyTimeoutExceeded` instead of starting further invocations. If the abandoned invocation later succeeds, its value is recorded as the applicator's `latestAppliedValue` although the application was reported as failed, so the next application is compared against the value the device actually holds, and while it is still running no application is skipped as already applied. An applicator timeout that is negative is now treated as zero, and one that is positive infinity or not a number as no timeout; on iOS 15 such a value crashed the first application. The \"Application timeout\" sections of the Basic and Automatic Processing Configuration Mode guides describe how an applicator should stop itself.
- A processing parameter reported the wrong value after an application was cancelled, or timed out, while its applicator was running. Cancelling the task that called `apply`, `load` or `synchronizeApplicator` returns at once with `CancellationError` and leaves the applicator running; the parameter published `.failed` straight away and kept its old `value`, although the applicator usually went on to succeed, so the device held the new value while `value` and `valuePublisher` reported the old one until the next application. The parameter's `updateState` now stays `.applying` until the applicator returns, and then reports `.applied` and publishes the value, or reports `.failed` with the applicator's own error. If another operation on the parameter has started in the meantime its states are not overwritten, and only the value is published, provided the device still holds it. With an applicator that never returns the state stays `.applying` until the next operation. An application that times out is followed up in the same way: it still fails at once with `applyTimeoutExceeded` and reports `.failed`, and if the applicator then returns successfully the parameter publishes the value and reports `.applied`; if the applicator throws, the reported timeout stands. A call cancelled or timed out before the applicator was invoked still fails at once without a value change. A call that timed out or was cancelled while waiting its turn can also no longer invoke the applicator afterwards. Because a suspension that timed out or whose call was cancelled can now be reported as having switched Sound Personalization or Voice Clarity off, the next resumption restores what the failed suspension recorded as on, and a suspension made before it keeps that record; likewise a suspension made while a failed resumption has switched the feature back on switches it off again; that recorded state is dropped once the feature is switched off on purpose or, for Sound Personalization, its value is loaded.
